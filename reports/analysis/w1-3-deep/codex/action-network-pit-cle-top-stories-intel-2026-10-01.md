- All findings remain hypotheses under the project's 2-SE threshold; none should be promoted to a pre-game rule from this one-game/article cluster.

## How the sources fit together

### Pittsburgh-led, lower-scoring script

**Supporting sources:** Sean Koerner, Frank Ammirante, Action Network Staff, Charles White.

- Koerner's game-preview model put spread and total near coin-flip territory but specifically selected Rodgers under 20.5 completions. He expects Pittsburgh to lead for roughly 44% of snaps and expects Warren to inherit a dominant workload with Rico Dowdle out.
- Ammirante's explicit 3-leg construction was Pittsburgh -2.5, Warren over 73.5 rushing, and Concepcion over 3.5 receptions (+330). His Warren premise is positive game script and 18+ carries.
- The staff article and White both chose the under, pointing to Cleveland's pressure defense, inefficient Browns rushing, and limited Cleveland offensive output. Their low-score premises overlap substantially.

**Interpretation:** This cluster is useful for organizing a scenario, but it contains correlated claims rather than independent confirmations. It needs final injury, weather, role, and price verification before any evaluation.

### Fewer Rodgers completions, but possible Metcalf splash efficiency

**Supporting sources:** Koerner and Sam Farley; Gallant separately supports a Metcalf TD outcome.

- Koerner's stated matchup split: Cleveland had the league's highest man rate and second-highest pressure rate; Rodgers' cited completion rate was materially lower versus man and under pressure. That is the basis for the completion under, not a passing-yard under.
- Farley framed Metcalf's 41.5-yard over as positive regression: 24 targets, fourth in league air yards, but only 98 receiving yards through three games. The cited path is a deep-ball connection rather than high catch volume.
- Gallant's Metcalf TD angle rests on end-zone usage and Cleveland's conceded receiver touchdowns, with a man/single-high framing.

**Interpretation:** These ideas can be logically compatible, but that does not establish a same-game-parlay price edge. A combination would need actual-book correlation/pricing review, not simple multiplication.

### Cleveland volume / target-distribution script

**Supporting sources:** staff article, Ammirante, Farley.

- The staff article selected Watson over 28.5 pass attempts and Fannin anytime TD, citing Browns pass volume, expected plays, and Fannin target share.
- Ammirante selected Concepcion over 3.5 catches as the short-area answer to Pittsburgh's rush.
- Farley selected Concepcion 5+ receptions, arguing that target volume is already there, and characterized it as a positive-regression play. He also selected Warren under 22.5 receiving yards because of Cleveland's RB receiving defense and expected carry rebalancing.

**Interpretation:** This is a plausible short-area volume story, but it partially pushes against a strict Pittsburgh-lead/low-play-count read. Keep it as a distinct scenario, rather than combining all positive signals into one stack.

### Cleveland-side contrarian / close-game view

**Supporting source:** Pablo Planovsky's prediction-market article.

- Planovsky called Cleveland moneyline the value side to monitor and advocated Cleveland +2.5 on the view that AFC North games frequently stay within one possession; he also preferred the under.
- The embedded Polymarket table was a snapshot (updated 2:59 PM PDT) of quarter-specific both-teams-to-score contracts, not a sportsbook line or a recommendation to transact.

**Interpretation:** This is the main directional conflict with the Pittsburgh-control cluster. It supports treating side confidence as low rather than using the repeated Pittsburgh narrative as consensus proof.
