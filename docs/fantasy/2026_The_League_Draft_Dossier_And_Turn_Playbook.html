The Official 9-Rule keeper system

1Round 1 & 2 Lockout — players drafted in the 1st or 2nd round cannot be kept (unless FA-reset before Week 11).
2-2 Round Discount — keeping a player costs a pick 2 rounds earlier than where they were drafted.
33-Year Tenure Limit — a player can't be kept more than 3 consecutive years.
4Active Lineup Requirement — must have started at least 1 week to be keeper-eligible.
5Round 10 FA Reset — in-season free-agent pickups (or players claimed after being dropped, before Week 11) reset to a Round 10 keeper cost.
6Round 14 Late-Round Floor — Round 14+ picks can only be kept at a Round 14 cost.
7Week 11 Roster Cutoff — waiver pickups after Week 11 aren't keeper-eligible.
8Trade Transfer — keeper cost and value travel with the player in a trade.
9Same-Round Collision — two keepers landing in the same round forces one to bump one round earlier.

⚠ The wildcard: No Talent Ass Clowns (slot 4, undeclared)

Nothing filed and nothing implied in league chat. Every turn-by-turn window below assumes this team keeps zero players (the most favorable case for you — the fullest possible board). If they declare 1-2 keepers before the deadline, the practical effect on your plan is small but real: it removes 1-2 more players from whatever round(s) they cost, which very slightly speeds up how fast the board in front of you thins in that range. It does not change your own pick numbers or change whether your Round 4 / Round 10 turns are live (those are fixed by your own keepers only).

What to actually do about it: nothing differently before the draft. Just don't be surprised if a round you expected to have slightly more depth (most likely somewhere in Rounds 4-10, based on how every other team in this league has costed their keepers) runs one or two names thinner than the windows below show. If it changes anything, it's a reason to lean slightly toward taking the safer/more proven name over the upside swing when two options in a window look close.

TE market note: Two of the four clear top-tier tight ends are already gone to keepers before you ever pick — Brock Bowers (Postino's Banditos, Rd 4) and Colston Loveland (Wailin Raylans, Rd 10). Trey McBride is the best remaining TE1 and, per the pick math below, has a real chance to still be sitting at your very first turn (1.12) — that's a notably better TE outcome than the raw ADP would suggest, precisely because two mid-round keeper locks quietly thinned the position above you.
