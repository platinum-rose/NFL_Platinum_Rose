# The League Recap Voice Guide

Last updated: 2026-09-21

Use this for future humorous weekly recaps for `2026 - The League`.

## Audience And Privacy

- The Week 2 recap was privately published to The League team managers for entertainment purposes only.
- Treat future recaps as private league entertainment, not public betting, fantasy advice, or official reporting.
- Do not include private Yahoo/API details, raw tokens, account IDs, or anything outside the league's normal shared fantasy context.
- Keep jokes friendly and league-room appropriate, using the manager roast levels in `THE_LEAGUE_MANAGER_PROFILES.md`.

## Voice Target

- Original deadpan surreal fantasy-football monologue.
- Inspired by broad one-liner mechanics, not by copying or impersonating a real comedian.
- The better lane is not terse robotic fragments; it is full comic sentences with a clear punchline and optional tag.
- Think: one person delivering a recap to the room, not a dashboard, newsletter grid, or quote-card page.

## What Worked

- The first draft's fuller comic language landed better than the later ultra-short sentence style.
- Amanda reported that this joke hit:
  - "Benching Mahomes is legal. So is putting ketchup on a steak. The law is not always enough."
- Absurd but concrete images worked well:
  - "shopping cart with one bad wheel"
  - "trying to remember his email password in a public library"
  - "remove the batteries from the remote control of his season"
  - "a small pamphlet explaining zoning laws"
  - "interpretive dance and nobody has to call a meeting"
- User-favorite joke structures:
  - kicker/QB distance: "two people standing very far apart professionally"
  - fantasy football as "regret to have a scoring format"
  - "two-point staring contest"
  - team-name/meta jokes like "the branding department is undefeated"
  - "normally" as a word fantasy football takes out "when someone needs to be hurt"
- Fantasy-specific absurdity worked best when the joke understood the game:
  - Starters vs bench points.
  - Pending players who have not played yet.
  - Yahoo win probability weirdness.
  - Bad lineup decisions looking fine in a blowout.
- Punchlines need to be easy to see, but should still feel like the same speaker.

## What Did Not Work

- Overly fragmented short sentences became robotic.
- Explaining the manager profile table in the intro felt like a preface, not a monologue.
- Over-designed HTML sections, boxes, and callouts broke the feeling of listening to a comic.
- Heavy bold or contrasting punchline colors felt like a second narrator yelling "joke here."
- Some surreal jokes failed when they did not connect clearly to fantasy football logic:
  - "tight end loophole"
  - "refrigerator from another room"
  - "errands to run"
  - "waiting for Monday in a suit"
  - "lineup that knows where the scissors are"
  - "Maybe we have been looking at the wrong adults"
- Do not roast a zero if the player has not played yet. A pending zero is not a failure.
- Avoid logic reversals. Example: "Most players need to touch the ball to lose points" was backwards; the intended joke is that most players need to touch the ball to gain points.
- Emphasis placement matters:
  - In "The bench was helpful. The lineup declined assistance," emphasize the second sentence.
  - In the Jared Goff/Tre Tucker bench joke, emphasize the final tag: "That is not a bench. That is a second team with worse parking."
  - "Two starters. Zero points." did not need bolding.
- Do not over-tag a joke that already landed. The interpretive-dance bench line should stand mostly on its own; extra tags weakened it.

## Recap Writing Rules

1. Start with a short monologue about The League and the absurdity of fantasy football.
2. Use manager profiles as flavor, not exposition. Do not declare the table at the top.
3. Keep matchups organized by score, but write like a comic working through the room.
4. Clearly separate starter points from bench points.
5. Mention bench points when they are funny, painful, or strategically relevant.
6. Vary bench-point phrasing. Repetition can be funny only when intentional.
7. Treat pending players carefully:
   - "His zero is not a zero. It is an appointment."
   - "Pending" means time has not finished being rude yet.
8. Prefer concrete images over abstract internet-roast language.
9. Use punchlines and tags:
   - Punchline: the main joke turn.
   - Tag/button: a smaller extra hit after the punchline.
10. Let the punchline land, then move on. Do not explain it.

## Formatting Rules

- Use one continuous monologue column.
- Use matchup headings, but avoid card layouts.
- Avoid boxed punchline callouts.
- Emphasis should be subtle and uniform:
  - Same speaker, same voice.
  - Do not use dramatically different bold typefaces or clashing colors.
  - Inline emphasis can use a mild highlight or shared neutral emphasis style.
- The comedy-club theme is promising:
  - Dark brick-wall style background.
  - Warm spotlight glow.
  - Cream text.
  - Amber/rose score accents.
  - No visual treatment that interrupts reading flow.
