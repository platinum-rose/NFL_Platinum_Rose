**Spread bets were the clear weak spot (30%, 20 legs)** — and it wasn't close. Week 1 (2026) was an unusually lopsided week across the board — six of our seven spread losses came in games decided by 14+ points. Worth a gut-check in future weeks: cheap underdog points are only +EV if the game is likely to stay competitive, and this week's actual results skewed toward blowouts more than the lines implied.

## Key Takeaways Heading Into Week 2

1. **Spread underdogs got run over this week.** If Week 2 lines look similarly tight on paper, don't assume competitive game scripts — check injury reports and situational spots more carefully before buying cheap points on a road/home underdog.
2. **Anytime-TD SGP stacks are close to a coinflip per leg.** A 3-leg all-TD parlay needs roughly 1-in-8 odds to land purely on independent probability — treat the payout accordingly and don't oversize stakes on these relative to player-yardage/reception props, which had comparable or better hit rates this week.
3. **Big favorites winning big doesn't always mean their skill-position props hit.** The DEN@KC game is the clearest example — KC won by 21, but the scoring and yardage got spread to unexpected names (Walker, Rice, Mahomes rushing) rather than the well-known Kelce/Sutton volume we targeted. When a team is a big favorite, consider whether the read is "this specific player gets volume" vs. "this team wins comfortably and volume could go anywhere."
4. **DraftKings Predictions board is worth tracking over a larger sample** — it outperformed this week, but on lower stakes/volume than Bookmaker.eu. Not enough data yet to size up confidently.
5. **Ticket construction matters as much as pick quality.** 46% leg-level hit rate but only 7% ticket win rate — the parlays that survived were the shorter ones or the ones that got lucky on every leg. Worth weighing more 2-3 leg tickets against fewer high-leg-count moonshots, especially on nights (like DEN@KC) where the read on which players get volume is less certain.
